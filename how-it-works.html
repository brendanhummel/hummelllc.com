How it works

A senior IT leader on a retainer: regular planning, real oversight, and scoped technical work — remote-first, with honest capacity.

What a retainer looks like

Quarterly and periodic planning

Regular planning sessions that keep your technology moving with your business — not stuck in reactive mode.

A technology roadmap

What to fix, what to upgrade, what to retire — prioritized against your budget and your risk.

Budget guidance

Straight talk about what your IT should cost and where the money is leaking to vendors.

Vendor oversight

Someone who watches the vendors on your behalf and asks the questions you don’t have time to.

Scoped work, transparently

Technical work — assessments, planning, policy writing, hands-on fixes — is part of the retainer or quoted per project. Everything is scoped up front: what’s included, what it costs, and when it’s done.

Honest capacity, year one: Hummel LLC runs at roughly 5 hours a week of founder time — evenings and weekends included — so engagements are few on purpose. You talk to the person doing the work, and that person is not overbooked.

Remote-first, with a local anchor

Meetings, reviews, and oversight are done remotely — that’s what keeps retainer pricing sensible anywhere in the country. Columbia, SC roots mean on-site visits are available where they genuinely help.

Start with a conversation

Engagement starts with an honest fit conversation — scope, capacity, and whether we’re actually the right fit for you.
